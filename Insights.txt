#Main discoveries (final results, after the full pipeline rework)

Capture rate:
- CR follows an exponential decay in penetration, not a logistic - a logistic's
  ceiling isn't its value at pen=0, so it can't match the calibrated pre-solar
  mean (U=1.00) the way the exp-decay does (R2 0.94 on annual data)
- observed CR fell from 1.01 (2005) to 0.89 (2025) - already ~12% erosion
  before any forward scenario is applied
- weekend cannibalisation is deepening, not constant: -0.011 discount in
  2005-10 vs -0.047 in 2022-25 - solar depresses weekend prices more now
  because weekend load is lower to begin with
- the floor L=0.20 is NOT identified by the data (R2 moves <0.002 across
  L in [0,0.6]) - it's an assumption, and it's the single biggest driver of
  the 2050 scenario spread

Revenue index (RI = production x capture rate):
- reference scenario (PNIEC penetration, baseline temp): RI falls from
  ~1025 (2026) to ~819 (2050), a 20% decline
- best case (current trend penetration, SSP1-2.6): only -11% by 2050
- worst case (ENTSO-E penetration, SSP5-8.5): -28% by 2050
- penetration is what actually moves this, not temperature: swapping the
  temperature scenario while holding penetration fixed changes the 2050 RI
  by well under 1 percentage point; swapping penetration while holding
  temperature fixed changes it by ~17 points. temperature is noise here,
  penetration is signal

Copula / dependence:
- CR-KT correlation is +0.31 (not the sign you'd first guess - eps_kt is
  sign-inverted, so this means high capture rate coincides with cloudy days,
  i.e. cannibalisation, as expected)
- t-copula (nu=17) beats Gaussian decisively (dAIC=-36.42) - the joint tails
  are fatter than a Gaussian dependence structure would give you, which
  matters because the hedge payoff lives in exactly that tail
- CR margin doesn't fully pass its own uniformity check (KS p=0.023) - kept
  as a recorded warning, not a blocker, same policy as the two other margins

Temperature & NOCT:
- warming trend is statistically real: +0.0585 C/yr since 2005
- AR(2) residuals are heavy-tailed (nu=6.9), NOT the pre-AR residuals (those
  looked heavy-tailed too but that was autocorrelation, not real fat tails -
  a nu-diverges-to-Gaussian check on the pre-AR series confirms this)
- daylight cell temperature runs ~2 C above the 24h mean in winter and
  ~6-7 C above it in summer - a single annual constant would understate
  summer derating specifically, which is exactly when the plant produces most

Hedge premium:
- fair premium per lot roughly triples over the horizon: ~3,535 EUR (2026)
  to ~9,610 EUR (2050), tracking the CR decline above
- seasonal notional dominates a flat one: same annual volume, lower premium,
  slightly BETTER variance reduction - a flat notional overpays for
  protection in winter (low production, low risk) and underpays in summer
  (high production, high exposure)
